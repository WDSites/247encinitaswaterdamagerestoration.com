How leaks travel between shops and homes

In a building with shared walls or stacked floors, a supply line that bursts in a ground-floor shop can wet the ceiling of the unit above. A dishwasher hose in an upstairs flat can drip into the shop below for days before a stain shows.

A broken line is a case for burst pipe water damage help, while rain that came in at street level fits storm water damage. If you are not sure which it is, describe what you see and we will sort it out.

Safety before cleanup

Stay out of water near outlets, panels or gas appliances. Cut power only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without standing in water. Never operate a breaker while standing in water.

If you smell gas, see sparks or hear buzzing, leave and call 911 or the utility before you call us. Sewage and floodwater are contaminated, so fans, ordinary vacuums and DIY drying are not enough. Do not touch anything that looks like a hazardous material.

Extraction and drying

A restoration crew checks how far the water has gone, usually with moisture meters, then pulls standing water with extraction equipment. Materials that cannot be dried, such as soaked carpet pad, may be removed.

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure, and readings are repeated until materials reach a dry standard. Ask the company about structural drying and how it documents readings.

Insurance and permits

Check your policy and ask your insurer what it covers. According to the California Department of Insurance, flood is typically left out of homeowners and commercial policies. Photos, notes and moisture readings help document a loss, but we cannot say what a claim will pay.
