Water heater leak cleanup is the removal of water that escaped from a tank, its fittings or its relief valve, plus the drying of everything that water touched. The plumber fixes or replaces the appliance. Our job is the wet floor, the soaked drywall and the baseboards around it.

If a heater is leaking in your Encinitas home right now, shut off the cold supply first and then call us. Dispatch is open 24/7. Same-day response is offered when available.

How a water heater ends up leaking

Not every leak means the tank has failed. A loose fitting, a dripping drain valve or a weeping connector can leak for weeks and cost little to fix. A rusted-through tank is different.

The temperature and pressure relief valve is a third source. It opens on purpose when pressure or heat climbs too high, and it sends water down a discharge pipe. If that pipe ends inside a closet or garage, a valve that keeps discharging can soak the floor for hours.

First-hour safety steps for a leaking water heater

Gas or electrical risk comes first

If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 before you call us. If water is reaching outlets, cords or the main panel, stay out of the wet area and call the electric utility or 911 first.

For an electric heater, turn off its breaker only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without standing in water. If you cannot, leave it and call for help.

  1. Close the cold supplyFind the shut-off valve on the cold pipe above the heater and turn it fully clockwise. If it is stuck or leaking, shut off the water at the meter or the main.
  2. Know your main shut-offCardiff, Old Encinitas, Leucadia and part of New Encinitas are served by the San Dieguito Water District, run by the City of Encinitas Utilities Department. The city points residents to its MyEncinitas map to find the provider for an address, so learn where your meter and main valve sit.
  3. Photograph before you move thingsTake pictures of the heater, the label and every wet surface for your insurer.

How we clean up after a heater leak

Heater water is usually clean supply water, so cleanup moves fast once the leak is stopped. Speed matters because drywall and insulation wick moisture upward.

  1. Extract standing waterSubmersible pumps and wet vacuums remove water from the closet floor, garage slab and adjoining rooms.
  2. Map the moistureMoisture meters read drywall, baseboards and subfloor. A thermal camera shows wet areas hidden behind finished surfaces.
  3. Remove what cannot drySaturated carpet pad, swollen baseboards and soft drywall at the base of the wall are cut out so the framing can dry.
  4. Set the drying equipmentAir movers and dehumidifiers run on the wet area, and we recheck readings until the materials reach a dry standard.

The industry reference for this work is the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for professional water damage restoration. You can ask any company whether its crews follow it. If the water spread past the closet, see emergency water removal.

Replacing the heater and what to ask the city

A plumber replaces the heater, and we dry and clean. The drying should not wait for the new unit, because wet framing keeps absorbing water.

Ask the Encinitas Building Division whether a replacement needs a permit. The division sits at 505 S. Vulcan Ave and can be reached at 760-633-2730.

California Health and Safety Code section 19211, as published in the state code, says all new and replacement water heaters, and all existing residential water heaters, must be braced, anchored or strapped against earthquake motion. Ask your plumber to confirm the new unit is strapped.

Check the plumber's license before you sign. The Contractors State License Board says a license is required for projects of $500 or more in labor and material, and the down payment should not exceed 10 percent or $1,000, whichever is less. You can look up a license at cslb.ca.gov or call 800-321-CSLB (2752).

What changes the cost of the cleanup

The cost follows the size of the wet area and the time it needs to dry. These factors move it most.

  • Water category. Clean supply water is simpler than water that has sat or picked up dirt from a garage floor.
  • Affected area. A closet floor and a closet plus a hallway and two bedrooms are very different jobs.
  • Drying days and equipment. More wet material means more air movers and dehumidifiers for more days.
  • Rebuild scope. Cutting out a section of baseboard and drywall is smaller than replacing flooring through several rooms.

Does insurance cover a water heater leak?

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered by a homeowners policy, so a tank that fails and floods a hallway may qualify. A slow drip that went unnoticed for months is often treated as gradual damage, which many policies exclude.

The California Department of Insurance notes that homeowners and commercial policies typically exclude flood, and that flood coverage comes from the federal National Flood Insurance Program. A burst heater is a plumbing event, not a flood, but the wording of your own policy decides. Check your policy or ask your insurer.

Mistakes to avoid and ways to prevent a repeat

  • Do not cap or block the relief valve discharge pipe. It is a safety device.
  • Do not relight a pilot or restore power to a heater that has been underwater until a professional looks at it.

A drain pan piped to a safe discharge point keeps a small leak off the floor. Look at the base of the tank each season when you check the garage or closet.
