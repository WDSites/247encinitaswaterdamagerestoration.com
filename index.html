Local causes

Where Water Damage Starts Around Encinitas

San Diego's Lindbergh Field gauge shows about 85 percent of the area's rain falling from November through March, with January the wettest month at 1.95 inches on the National Weather Service long-term table. That is a good reason to check roof flashing, gutters and window seals in October, because a slow leak found early is a patch, and one found in January is a stained ceiling.

The City of Encinitas hazard mitigation plan names the low-lying streets of Leucadia, the Cardiff coastline near Restaurant Row and Escondido, Encinitas and Cottonwood Creeks as flood-prone. It also records that heavy rain in 2005 closed the intersection of El Camino Real and Leucadia Boulevard. If you live near those areas, check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask your insurer about flood coverage before the next storm.

Utilities differ block by block. The city's San Dieguito Water District serves Cardiff, Old Encinitas, Leucadia and part of New Encinitas, and Leucadia Wastewater District handles sewer for Leucadia and much of New Encinitas. When a sewer line backs up into a house, the private lateral between the building and the street main belongs to the owner, so cleanup begins with that pipe and a call to the right agency.

Emergency steps

What To Do Before We Arrive

An emergency in a wet house is mostly about the next hour. These steps limit the damage while dispatch arranges the work, and none of them require special tools.

  1. Stop the sourceClose the fixture valve or the main water valve. If the water is rain or a flood, move to higher ground and keep people and pets away.
  2. Cut power only if it is safeTurn off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without standing in water. Otherwise stay out and call the utility or 911 first.
  3. Lift what you canRaise furniture legs onto blocks, pull rugs and lift valuables, electronics and paperwork off wet floors.
  4. Take photosPhotograph the source, the wet rooms and damaged belongings before anything moves. Your insurer will ask for them.
  5. Call dispatchCall (760) 769-1159 and describe what you see. We handle emergencies at night, on weekends and on holidays.

Water and electricity

If water is near outlets, a panel or a gas appliance, or if sewage is involved, stay out of the area. Call the utility or 911 for any electrical or gas risk before you call the restoration line.

Cost factors

What Changes the Cost of Water Damage Work

No honest company can quote a water damage job without seeing it. These are the factors that move the number most, which is why we explain the scope before work starts.

Cost factors for water damage restoration
FactorWhy it matters
Water categoryClean supply-line water dries differently from Category 2 or 3 water, which needs removal of porous materials and sanitizing.
Affected areaMore square footage and more rooms mean more equipment, more drying days and more materials to replace.
Materials involvedHardwood, carpet pad, drywall and insulation each have different salvage limits.
Drying days and equipmentAir movers and dehumidifiers run until moisture readings reach the dry standard, so the timeline sets the cost.
Rebuild scopePainting a wall is far less work than replacing cabinets, flooring and insulation.

Insurance

Insurance and Water Damage Claims

Documentation your insurer will ask for

We record moisture readings, photograph the affected rooms and write an itemized scope of work, so your adjuster can see what happened and what the repair involves.

Coverage depends on your policy. Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and flood damage usually needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy or ask your insurer.

The California Department of Insurance notes that homeowners policies typically exclude flood, mudslide and debris flow, and that flood coverage comes through the federal National Flood Insurance Program, which usually takes effect 30 days after purchase. Neighborhoods

Encinitas Neighborhoods We Serve

Old Encinitas is a small beachside area that mixes businesses and housing. In mixed blocks a leak in one unit often reaches the next, so tell the property manager early and photograph the damage before any cleanup.

New Encinitas, which includes Village Park, shares ZIP 92024 with much of the city. The city's water map shows which provider serves your address, and that is worth knowing before a shut-off emergency.

Leucadia sits along the northern coast, and the city's hazard plan records a chronic history of rainwater flooding near North Coast Highway 101 and North Vulcan Avenue. Residents there should keep flood coverage in mind when reviewing a policy.

Cardiff-by-the-Sea, ZIP 92007, is the southernmost oceanfront community and is home to the Mira Costa College San Elijo Campus. The hazard plan names the Cardiff coastline near Restaurant Row as flood-prone, so storm cleanup calls from there are common in winter.

Olivenhain is the semi-rural eastern area, mostly single-family homes, linked to Rancho Santa Fe by Encinitas Boulevard. The city lists San Dieguito Water District for other neighborhoods, so use the city's MyEncinitas map to confirm which provider serves an Olivenhain address.
