First-hour safety steps

Before you call anyone

Stay out of standing water near outlets, panels and gas appliances. Turn power off only if the panel is dry and you can get to it without wading, and keep your hands off breakers while your feet are wet.

If you smell gas or see sparks, leave and call 911 or the utility first. Treat sewage and floodwater as contaminated: household fans, ordinary vacuums and do-it-yourself drying will not make it safe.

  1. Shut off the water at the fixture valve or the main, if you can do so safely.
  2. Photograph the source, the water line on walls and any damaged belongings before moving things.
  3. Tell neighbors below or beside you, since the water may reach them.
  4. Call (760) 769-1159 and describe the damage.

Which agency serves your address

The City of Encinitas Utilities page says San Dieguito Water District serves part of New Encinitas. The city's wastewater page says Leucadia Wastewater District, a separate agency, serves much of New Encinitas. Because the provider depends on the address, use the city's MyEncinitas map to look yours up, and find your main water shut-off now.

On the sewage side, the city assigns the private lateral, the line between your building and the street main, to the property owner. Once sewage reaches the main, the agency is accountable. See sewage backup cleanup.

What the crew does to extract and dry

The crew that comes out finds the source and checks what the water touched with moisture meters. Standing water is pulled out with pumps or extraction units. Soaked carpet pad or soft drywall may be cut out.

Air movers and dehumidifiers then dry floors and wall cavities, and readings are repeated until the materials reach a dry standard. Read more on structural drying. Mold needs moisture, so ask whether the state licenses mold work if you see growth later.

Documenting the loss for insurance

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not, so check your policy and ask your insurer. Photos, dated notes and moisture readings from the crew help document what happened.

California requires a contractor license for projects of $500 or more. The down payment cap is 10 percent or $1,000, whichever is less. Check a license at cslb.ca.gov.
