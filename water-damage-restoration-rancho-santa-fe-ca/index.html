Large properties and where water hides

Rancho Santa Fe is a census-designated place of estate homes on large lots, and that layout shapes water damage. On a large property, the line between the meter and the house can be long, and a house with several wings has more walls where a pipe can fail out of sight. A slow leak may soak a guest room floor for days before anyone steps on it.

That is why a call from Rancho Santa Fe often starts with a question about where the water is coming from. A dripping ceiling below a bathroom, a swollen baseboard in a hallway or a damp smell in a pool house all point to different sources. The water mitigation team works from the wettest spot outward with moisture meters to find the real edge of the damage.

Water supply and permits in Rancho Santa Fe

Santa Fe Irrigation District supplies Rancho Santa Fe, along with Solana Beach and Fairbanks Ranch. The district gets about 30 percent of its potable water from Lake Hodges and about 70 percent from the San Diego County Water Authority. A pressure problem or a leak at the meter belongs with the district, so confirm the district for your parcel before you report it.

Rancho Santa Fe is an unincorporated community, so building permits for most addresses go through San Diego County Planning and Development Services at 5510 Overland Avenue, Suite 310, San Diego, CA 92123, (858) 694-8985. Their staff can say whether a repair needs one. Fairbanks Ranch is mentioned for orientation only, since it shares the same water district.

ZIPs 92067 and 92091

ZIP 92067 is the main ZIP for Rancho Santa Fe, with 9,344 residents in the 2020 Census, and it mixes post office boxes with some street delivery. In 92067, a burst line in a guest wing is typically emergency water removal followed by drying, and our emergency water removal crews pull the standing water out first.

ZIP 92091 is a smaller standard ZIP with 1,571 residents. After water has been extracted from a floor or a wall there, water damage cleanup takes over, which means removing soaked materials and sanitizing what the water touched. Larger losses in either ZIP may involve flood damage restoration if storm water entered.

While you wait for the crew: protect the property

Find the right valve

A large property may have a main valve and several zone valves. Close the main if you cannot tell which line is failing.

Power and pumps

Switch off breakers for wet rooms from a dry panel. Leave pump and pool equipment alone if water is near it.

Protect fine finishes

Lift art, rugs and furniture off wet floors. Place foil or plastic under furniture legs on wood and stone.

Keep contaminated water away

Water from a toilet or drain is unsafe to touch. Keep children and pets out of that wing.

Record for your insurer

Photograph the source, the water line and affected items. Then call (760) 769-1159 so we know what to bring.
